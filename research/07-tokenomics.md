# 07 — Token Economics

> "The invisible hand of crypto — how tokens are designed to capture value, incentivize behavior, and sustain ecosystems."

## Why This Canvas Matters

Every crypto project has a token. Understanding tokenomics is the difference between a beginner who buys based on price and an intermediate user who evaluates supply schedules, vesting cliffs, and value accrual. This is one of the most practical knowledge areas.

---

## Canvas Structure

### Main Node: "Token Economics"
*"How tokens are designed, distributed, and engineered to create sustainable economic systems"*

### Sub-Nodes (6 categories):

#### 1. Token Types & Classification
- **Nodes**:
  - Utility tokens (access to a service — ETH for gas, LINK for oracle payments)
  - Governance tokens (voting rights — UNI, AAVE, ARB)
  - Security tokens (represent ownership — regulated)
  - Stablecoins (pegged value — covered in DeFi)
  - NFTs (unique tokens — covered in Applications)
  - Memecoins (community/speculation-driven — DOGE, PEPE, WIF)
  - Gas tokens (pay for computation — ETH, SOL, AVAX)
  - LP tokens (represent liquidity positions)
  - Liquid staking tokens (stETH, jitoSOL — wrapped staking positions)

#### 2. Token Distribution & Launch
- **Nodes**:
  - ICO era (2017 — raise funds by selling tokens)
  - IEO/IDO (exchange/DEX-based launches)
  - Fair launch (no pre-mine, no VC allocation — Bitcoin model)
  - Airdrops (distribute to early users — Uniswap, Arbitrum, Optimism)
  - Points systems (modern airdrop meta — Blast, EigenLayer)
  - Launchpads (Binance Launchpad, Jupiter LFG)
  - Typical allocation: Team 15-20%, Investors 20-30%, Community 30-50%, Treasury 10-20%
  - Vesting schedules (cliffs, linear unlock, milestone-based)

#### 3. Supply Mechanics
- **Nodes**:
  - Fixed supply (Bitcoin — 21M cap, deflationary by nature)
  - Inflationary (Ethereum PoS — ~0.5% annual issuance, partially offset by burns)
  - Burn mechanics (EIP-1559 base fee burn, BNB quarterly burns)
  - Halving events (Bitcoin halving every ~4 years — supply shock)
  - Emission schedules (how new tokens enter circulation)
  - Circulating vs total vs max supply (why they matter)
  - FDV (Fully Diluted Valuation) vs market cap

#### 4. Value Accrual Models
- **Nodes**:
  - Fee distribution (protocol fees → token holders — Uniswap fee switch, MakerDAO surplus)
  - Buy-and-burn (protocol buys tokens with revenue and burns — like stock buybacks)
  - Revenue sharing (real yield — GMX, Trader Joe)
  - Staking rewards (inflationary but incentivizes security)
  - ve-Token model (vote-escrow — lock tokens for boosted yield and governance — Curve's veCRV)
  - Bribing markets (Convex/Votium — pay token holders to vote for your pool)
  - Real yield vs ponzi yield (sustainable vs unsustainable)

#### 5. Token Design Patterns
- **Nodes**:
  - Dual-token models (play token + governance token — Axie's SLP/AXS)
  - Rebasing tokens (auto-adjust supply — Ampleforth, OHM)
  - Bonding curves (price is a function of supply — continuous token models)
  - xToken pattern (deposit token, receive yield-bearing version — xSUSHI)
  - Soulbound tokens (non-transferable — reputation, credentials)
  - Points → token conversions (modern meta)

#### 6. Analyzing Tokenomics (Practical Guide)
- **Nodes**:
  - Read the token unlock schedule (TokenUnlocks.app)
  - Check insider allocation vs community
  - Evaluate value accrual (does the token capture protocol revenue?)
  - Inflation rate vs staking yield (real yield)
  - Supply overhang (upcoming unlocks that create sell pressure)
  - Revenue multiples (P/E ratio for tokens — Token Terminal)
