## 1. MiCA (Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation) — Complete Summary

### Overview
MiCA is the EU's comprehensive crypto regulation framework — the most detailed crypto law in the world.

### Timeline
| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| **Sep 2020** | European Commission proposes MiCA |
| **Jun 2023** | MiCA published in EU Official Journal |
| **Jun 2024** | Stablecoin provisions take effect (Title III & IV) |
| **Dec 2024** | Full MiCA takes effect (all provisions) |
| **Jan 2025** | Full enforcement begins across all 27 EU member states |
| **Nov 2025** | €540M+ in fines issued for non-compliance |

### Key Requirements

#### For Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASPs)
| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| **Authorization** | Must be licensed in at least one EU member state |
| **Passporting** | Once licensed in one country, can operate across all 27 EU states |
| **Capital requirements** | €50K-€150K minimum (depending on services) |
| **Governance** | Fit-and-proper requirements for management |
| **Custody** | Segregation of client assets, insurance/guarantees |
| **AML/KYC** | Full compliance with EU Anti-Money Laundering Directive |
| **White paper** | Mandatory for token issuance (like a prospectus lite) |

#### For Stablecoins
| Token Type | Requirements |
|-----------|-------------|
| **E-Money Tokens (EMT)** | Pegged to single fiat. Must be issued by credit institution or e-money institution. 100% reserve in safe assets. Redeemable at par at any time. |
| **Asset-Referenced Tokens (ART)** | Pegged to basket/commodity. Stricter rules. Reserve requirements. Volume caps for "significant" ARTs. |
| **Volume caps** | "Significant" stablecoins (>€1M daily tx, >€200M market cap) face additional requirements |

### Impact on Industry
- **USDT concerns**: Tether does not fully comply with MiCA (no EU banking license). Some EU exchanges delisting USDT.
- **USDC advantage**: Circle obtained MiCA license — USDC is MiCA-compliant
- **Compliance costs**: €500K-€2M+ for initial licensing, €200K-€500K/yr ongoing
- **Enforcement**: 540M€+ in fines issued by November 2025 — EU is serious
- **Market structure shift**: Many smaller exchanges exiting EU or consolidating

## 2. GENIUS Act (US Stablecoin Framework)

### Overview
The Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for US Stablecoins (GENIUS) Act — the first comprehensive US stablecoin law.

### Key Provisions
| Provision | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Issuer types** | Banks, credit unions, and licensed non-bank entities can issue stablecoins |
| **Reserve requirements** | 1:1 backing with US Treasuries, cash, central bank deposits, or short-term T-bills |
| **Audits** | Monthly reserve attestations, annual audits by registered accounting firm |
| **Redemption** | Must honor redemption requests at par within 1 business day |
| **Consumer protection** | Stablecoin holders have priority claim in bankruptcy (super-priority) |
| **Federal vs State** | Issuers <$10B market cap can be state-regulated; >$10B require federal oversight (Fed or OCC) |
| **Foreign issuers** | Must comply with comparable standards or be banned from US markets |
| **Algorithmic stablecoins** | Essentially banned — must have hard-asset reserves |

### Status
| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| Feb 2025 | Introduced in Senate (bipartisan — Lummis + Gillibrand) |
| May 2025 | Passed Senate Banking Committee |
| Jul 2025 | Signed into law by President |
| Jan 2026 | Implementation rules published by Fed/OCC |
| Mid 2026 | Full compliance deadline for existing issuers |

### Impact
- **Tether**: Must comply or face US market restrictions. Tether's opacity around reserves is the key concern.
- **Circle (USDC)**: Already compliant — stands to gain market share
- **Banks entering crypto**: JPMorgan, Bank of America exploring stablecoin issuance
- **PayPal (PYUSD)**: Already compliant as a licensed entity

## 5. Tornado Cash Legal Timeline

(See also 08-privacy-research.md for detailed timeline)

### Summary of Key Events
| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|-------------|
| Aug 2022 | OFAC sanctions Tornado Cash | First time open-source smart contracts sanctioned |
| Aug 2022 | Alexey Pertsev arrested (Netherlands) | Developer liability for privacy tools |
| Mar 2023 | Roman Storm arrested (US) | US enforcement action against developer |
| Nov 2024 | **5th Circuit rules OFAC overstepped** | Immutable code ≠ sanctionable property. Major win. |
| Jan 2025 | OFAC removes Tornado Cash from SDN list | Sanctions officially lifted |
| Mar 2025 | DOJ drops charges against Roman Storm | US government retreats |
| May 2024 | Pertsev convicted in Netherlands (5yr 4mo) | Divergent outcome — EU treats differently |

### Legal Precedent Established
- **US**: Code is not property of a foreign national → immutable smart contracts cannot be sanctioned
- **Netherlands**: Developer liability exists — building tools used for money laundering can result in criminal prosecution
- **Divergence**: US and EU taking different approaches to developer liability
- **Impact**: Chilling effect on privacy development somewhat reversed by 5th Circuit ruling

## 6. SEC Enforcement Actions (Major Cases)

| Date | Case | Outcome | Significance |
|------|------|---------|-------------|
| **Dec 2020** | SEC v. Ripple (XRP) | Partial victory for Ripple (2023) — XRP not a security in secondary market sales | Landmark: programmatic sales ≠ securities |
| **Jun 2023** | SEC v. Coinbase | Ongoing → likely settlement/resolution under new SEC leadership | Challenged exchange model |
| **Jun 2023** | SEC v. Binance | Settled (2024) — $4.3B fine, CZ banned from management | Largest crypto enforcement action |
| **Nov 2023** | SEC v. Kraken | Settled — $30M fine, shut down staking service | Staking-as-a-service as securities |
| **2023** | SEC v. Terraform Labs (LUNA) | Settled (2024) — $4.5B judgment | Algorithmic stablecoin fraud |
| **2024** | SEC v. Uniswap Labs | Wells Notice → case dropped under new SEC leadership (2025) | DEX regulation retreat |
| **2024** | SEC v. ConsenSys (MetaMask) | Case dropped (2025) | Wallet/swap provider not a broker |
| **2024** | SEC v. OpenSea | Wells Notice → withdrawn (2025) | NFT marketplace not a securities exchange |

### SEC Policy Shift (2025+)
- New SEC Chair (Paul Atkins) adopted "rules-first" approach vs previous "enforcement-first"
- Multiple cases dropped or settled favorably for crypto
- SAB 121 rescinded — banks can now custody crypto without balance sheet impact
- Staff guidance: most tokens are NOT securities if sufficiently decentralized
- **Crypto Task Force** formed to create clear regulatory framework

## 7. DeFi Regulation Proposals and Challenges

### The Core Challenge
> How do you regulate a smart contract that runs autonomously, has no CEO, no office, and no employees?

### Current Approaches

| Approach | Jurisdiction | Details |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Regulate the frontend** | US (proposed) | DeFi protocols are permissionless, but frontends can be regulated (geo-blocking, KYC) |
| **Regulate the DAO** | US (CFTC v. Ooki DAO) | Token holders = liable partners of an unincorporated association |
| **Infrastructure providers** | EU (MiCA + extensions) | Regulate oracles, RPCs, bridges as CASPs |
| **Tax reporting** | US (IRS) | DeFi "brokers" must report transactions (1099 requirements) — challenged in court |
| **AML/KYC at protocol level** | FATF recommendation | Travel Rule applied to DeFi — technically challenging |

### Key Regulatory Challenges
1. **Decentralization spectrum**: Is a "DeFi" protocol with a multisig admin key really decentralized?
2. **No off switch**: Immutable contracts can't be shut down (Tornado Cash proved this)
3. **Pseudonymity**: Blockchain addresses are pseudonymous, making KYC at protocol level difficult
4. **Global by default**: DeFi is borderless — regulations in one country can't stop access from another
5. **Composability**: Regulating one protocol affects all protocols that compose with it

### Emerging Consensus
- **Compliant frontends + permissionless protocols**: The likely endgame
- Frontend operators (like Uniswap Labs) implement KYC/geo-blocking
- Smart contracts remain permissionless and immutable
- "Regulatory sandboxes" for DeFi experimentation (UK, Singapore, Dubai)

## 8. Compliance Costs for Crypto Startups

### MiCA Licensing Costs (EU)

| Category | Estimated Cost |
|----------|---------------|
| **Legal preparation** | €100K-€300K |
| **Technology/compliance systems** | €200K-€500K |
| **Application fees** | €10K-€50K (varies by member state) |
| **Capital requirements** | €50K-€150K (held in reserve) |
| **Ongoing compliance (annual)** | €200K-€500K (staff, reporting, audits) |
| **Total Year 1** | €500K-€2M+ |

### US Compliance Costs

| Category | Estimated Cost |
|----------|---------------|
| **Money Transmitter License (MTL)** | $50K-$500K per state (need 50 states) |
| **BitLicense (New York)** | $100K+ in application costs |
| **Legal counsel** | $200K-$1M/yr |
| **Compliance staff** | $300K-$500K/yr (2-3 compliance officers) |
| **AML/KYC systems** | $100K-$300K/yr (Chainalysis, Elliptic, etc.) |
| **Audits** | $100K-$300K/yr |
| **Total Year 1** | $1M-$3M+ |

### Other Jurisdictions
| Jurisdiction | Licensing Cost | Annual Compliance |
|-------------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Dubai (VARA)** | $50K-$200K | $100K-$300K/yr |
| **Singapore (MAS)** | $50K-$150K | $150K-$400K/yr |
| **Hong Kong** | $100K-$300K | $200K-$500K/yr |
| **Switzerland** | $100K-$500K | $200K-$500K/yr |

### Impact on Startups
- **Barrier to entry**: Only well-funded startups can afford compliance ($1M+ year 1)
- **Favors incumbents**: Coinbase, Binance, Kraken can absorb costs; small teams cannot
- **Regulatory arbitrage**: Many startups choose Dubai/Singapore over US/EU for lower costs
- **"Compliance-first" trend**: Investors now require regulatory strategy before funding
